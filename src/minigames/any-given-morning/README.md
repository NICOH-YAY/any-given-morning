# Any Given Morning

Motivator: Meaning. Author: NICOH-YAY.

## Mechanic

You wake up on a small farm or by the sea. Walk with WASD or the arrow keys and press E to talk, pick things up and give them away. People ask for small favors, like a flower for a windowsill or bait for a fishing line. The Mortality switch on the menu is the toggle. When it is on, every life ends 16 to 24 seconds in, whatever you are doing, and you wake up somewhere new. Five lives make a round. When it is off, you get one 100 second day with the same people and favors.

## Dynamic

With Mortality on, the playtester spent the first lives talking to whoever was nearby. By the second life they had worked out that you die no matter what. By the fourth they were trying to help at least one person before the end, and sometimes died halfway through a favor. With Mortality off, they wandered, talked to a few people and did not try to finish anything.

## Aesthetic

With Mortality on, the deaths surprised them, and the meteor was their favorite. Dying in the middle of helping someone felt bad, and the short lives felt rushed. They said the rush also made one small action for someone feel meaningful. With Mortality off, they did not feel like doing much.

## Sources

> Visible impact, as when World of Warcraft changes the world after a player's quests, so that "your actions are having a significant impact on the world" (McGonigal 2011, ch. 3).

A short life pushed the playtester toward one visible act for one person, and a finished favor changes the place, such as a lantern that gets its oil. Art is Kenney's Tiny Farm (CC0) plus three Higgsfield sprites. Codex suggested seeding worlds, lifespans and deaths on separate streams. I kept it, so nothing a player does can move the moment of death.
